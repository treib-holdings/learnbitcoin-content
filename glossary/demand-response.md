Demand response is the set of grid programs that pay large electricity users to cut their usage when the grid is stressed. In Texas, for example, the grid operator ERCOT strikes deals with flexible power buyers and pays them to cut back so the grid isn't overstressed when air conditioners are running flat out. The grid gets load it can shed at the worst moments; the customer gets paid for being flexible.

Bitcoin [miners](/glossary/mining) are among those buyers. Riot Platforms, a miner with sites in Texas, says in its annual report that mining load can be quickly curtailed, and that the payments offset the mining revenue it gives up while switched off. Even miners without a deal with the grid sometimes power down on their own when prices spike.

## The Texas receipt

Riot's filings show how this plays out on ERCOT, the Texas grid. In August 2023, during a heat wave, Riot reported **$31.7 million** in credits for a single month: $24.2 million from selling power it had bought under long-term contracts back to the grid at spot prices, plus $7.4 million in demand-response credits. The 333 bitcoin it mined that month were worth about $8.9 million at the end of the month. During the periods of peak demand, Riot cut its power use by more than 95 percent.

Riot's annual report for 2023 describes three ways powering down pays off for it:

- **Selling power back.** Riot buys power at a fixed price under a long-term contract, so when spot prices spike it can stop mining and sell the unused power back to the grid.
- **Ancillary services.** Riot bids to sell ERCOT the right to switch off part of its load on command, and is paid whether or not ERCOT ever calls on it.
- **Peak avoidance.** ERCOT's Four Coincident Peak (4CP) program looks at the single highest-demand interval in each of four summer months, June through September. Riot powered down at those peaks in 2022, which substantially cut the transmission charges on its 2023 power bills.

## The debate

Critics see these payments differently. In October 2022, eight members of Congress, including Senators Elizabeth Warren and Ed Markey, wrote to ERCOT calling its demand-response payments to miners subsidies that come at the expense of ratepayers, and said mining puts a strain on the grid. After Riot's August 2023 numbers came out, Luke Metzger, executive director of the group Environment Texas, called the $31.7 million absurd, said other Texans weren't paid for cutting their own use, and argued that ERCOT should pay households to conserve instead. A 2023 Texas bill, SB 1751, would have capped how much miners could take part in these programs. It passed the state Senate and died in a House committee.

Riot's side, in its filings, is that its curtailment helped lower total demand on the grid during the heat wave and helped keep consumers' power on, and that it bids against other market participants to sell these services to ERCOT.
