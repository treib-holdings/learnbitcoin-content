[BIP-361](https://github.com/bitcoin/bips/blob/master/bip-0361.mediawiki) - *"Post Quantum Migration and Legacy Signature Sunset"* - is a draft proposal by Jameson Lopp, Christian Papathanasiou, Ian Smith, Joe Ross, Steve Vaile, and Pierre-Luc Dallaire-Demers, assigned its number on February 11, 2026 and merged into the BIPs repository in April 2026. It is the migration framework under discussion for [Post-Quantum Bitcoin](/glossary/post-quantum-bitcoin): the only categorical cryptographic threat that exists for Bitcoin's current signature schemes is a sufficiently powerful quantum computer, and BIP-361 is one proposal for how the network would respond.

## The problem

[ECDSA](/glossary/ecdsa-elliptic-curve-digital-signature-algorithm) and [Schnorr](/glossary/schnorr-signature) signatures rely on the discrete logarithm problem in elliptic-curve groups being computationally hard. A large-enough quantum computer running [Shor's algorithm](/glossary/shors-algorithm) breaks that assumption. Any UTXO whose [public key](/glossary/public-key) has been revealed on-chain becomes spendable by anyone with such a machine.

Public keys are revealed in three main scenarios:

- **[P2PK outputs](/glossary/p2pk-pay-public-key)** (pay-to-pubkey, used in early Bitcoin): the pubkey is in the output script itself, exposed from the moment the output is created. Mostly Satoshi-era coins.
- **[Address reuse](/glossary/address-reuse)**: any address that has *already sent* a transaction has revealed its pubkey in the spending tx. Receive once to a hashed address type and never spend, and the pubkey stays hidden. Spend once, and the pubkey is permanently public for that address.
- **[P2TR outputs](/glossary/taproot)** (Taproot): the bech32m address is the tweaked public key itself, so every Taproot output is exposed from the moment it is created, spent or not.

The BIP says that as of March 1, 2026, **over 34% of all bitcoin** had revealed a public key on chain. That includes much of Satoshi's stash, dormant whale wallets, and any address that's been reused.

## The two-phase migration

The proposal splits the migration into staged consensus changes:

**Phase A - Stop the bleeding.** Disallow *sending* funds to quantum-vulnerable address types. New transactions must use a quantum-resistant output type (the BIP is signature-scheme agnostic and assumes a separate proposal lands a PQ output type). Holders can still spend from vulnerable addresses, but only *to* safe ones. The draft puts Phase A 160,000 blocks (about three years) after BIP-361 activates.

**Phase B - Sunset legacy signatures.** Two years after Phase A, about five years after BIP-361 itself activates, ECDSA and Schnorr spends are restricted. Funds in vulnerable addresses can only be moved via a **quantum-safe rescue protocol** - one that proves something the real owner knows and a quantum attacker holding only the public key does not. The BIP's example is [BIP-32](/glossary/bip-32) hardened derivation: prove knowledge of the parent extended private key above a hardened step, which a quantum attacker who only recovered the on-chain key would still not have.

The five-year runway exists because, in the authors' framing, *"coordinating wallets, exchanges, miners and custodians historically takes years."*

## What this is and isn't

**Is:** a draft BIP. Would deploy as a [soft fork](/glossary/soft-fork) if it advances. Currently in the open-discussion phase.

**Isn't:** activated, scheduled, or consensus. The community has not agreed on which quantum-resistant signature scheme to use. Candidates include lattice-based ([ML-DSA](/glossary/ml-dsa-dilithium), standardized by NIST in August 2024 as FIPS 204) and hash-based ([SLH-DSA](/glossary/slh-dsa-sphincs-plus), FIPS 205) schemes, each with different trade-offs in signature size, verification cost, and assumed security; BIP-360 dropped the isogeny-based SQIsign from consideration in January 2025 over performance.

The BIP's authors cite estimates of a [cryptographically-relevant quantum computer (CRQC)](/glossary/crqc-cryptographically-relevant-quantum-computer) "as early as 2027-2030", sourced to McKinsey's 2025 Quantum Technology Monitor; the quantum chapter linked below goes through the timelines in more depth. The BIP exists to start the multi-year coordination process *before* the threat is acute, not after.

## The Satoshi question

The two-phase mechanism implicitly addresses what to do about dormant pre-quantum coins. Under Phase B, a P2PK output (including [Satoshi's](/glossary/satoshi-nakamoto) known holdings) can no longer be spent with a plain ECDSA signature. The BIP's authors say no rescue protocol is currently believed possible for P2PK, because no knowledge asymmetry between the owner and a quantum attacker is known; they instead support a separate "Hourglass" proposal for P2PK spends, provided it activates before Phase B. Without one, those coins would be locked for good.

The BIP lists this as a benefit rather than a side effect ("supply discipline": abandoned keys that never migrate stay unspendable), and it is an unavoidable consequence: the same rule that protects vulnerable holders from quantum theft also strands the original holdings of anyone whose keys are unavailable. Whether the network views that as a feature or a bug is part of why this is still a draft.

See the [Quantum and Bitcoin rabbit hole](/rabbit-hole/quantum-and-bitcoin) for the measured exposure this framework would migrate - and why mass migration in a panic would be the worst response.
