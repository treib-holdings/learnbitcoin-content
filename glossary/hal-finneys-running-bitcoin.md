"Running bitcoin." Two words, posted by Hal Finney on Twitter on January 10, 2009. It's the most quietly important tweet in Bitcoin's history.

The context: Bitcoin had launched a week earlier when [Satoshi](/glossary/satoshi-nakamoto) mined the [genesis block](/glossary/genesis-block). Satoshi had announced Bitcoin v0.1 on the Cryptography Mailing List on January 8, 2009. Hal Finney - a legendary cryptographer who'd worked on PGP and authored RPOW (an earlier reusable-proof-of-work system) - downloaded the software, ran it, and tweeted those two words.

He was the second person ever known to run Bitcoin. On January 12, 2009, Satoshi sent him 10 BTC in block 170, the first known Bitcoin transaction from one person to another. He corresponded with Satoshi over email about bugs and improvements through much of 2009.

Hal was diagnosed with ALS later that year. As the disease progressed, he wrote a Bitcoin Talk forum post in 2013 reflecting on his journey, his hope for Bitcoin's future, and his decision to keep his BTC. He died in 2014. His body was cryonically preserved by the Alcor Life Extension Foundation - a choice consistent with the broader transhumanist current that ran through the early cypherpunk community.

"Running bitcoin" the tweet has become a cultural touchstone in Bitcoin. The phrase appears on T-shirts, conference banners, and the wall of pretty much every node-runner's home setup. It captures, in the simplest possible language, what early Bitcoiners did: download the software, run a node, contribute to the network. That ethos hasn't changed.

Hal's [Bitcoin Talk profile](https://bitcointalk.org/index.php?action=profile;u=2436) is still up. In his 2013 post [Bitcoin and me](https://bitcointalk.org/index.php?topic=155054.0), he wrote that he had mined several blocks in the first days and later moved his coins to an offline wallet, hoping they would be worth something to his heirs. See [Satoshi Nakamoto](/glossary/satoshi-nakamoto) for the only other person who can plausibly claim that level of early Bitcoin credibility.
