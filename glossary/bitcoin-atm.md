A Bitcoin ATM is a kiosk, often in a shop or gas station, that sells bitcoin for cash or a card payment. You identify yourself (a phone number can be enough), show it the QR code of an [address](/glossary/address) and put your money in. The bitcoin goes to that address, whoever it belongs to, and cannot be called back.

- **What it costs.** FinCEN, a US Treasury office, puts the fees at 7 to 20 percent. In lawsuits announced in February 2025, Iowa's Attorney General said the state's two largest operators charged up to 23 percent and up to 21.9 percent, and that the second had kept 10.86 percent of all the money put through its Iowa machines from 2021 to mid-2024.
- **How scammers use it.** The FBI says criminals give victims "detailed instructions": how to withdraw cash, find a kiosk and send the money. In 2025 it received 13,460 complaints that involved a kiosk, with about $389 million lost, not all of it through a machine. The FTC put the median reported loss at $10,000 in the first half of 2024.
- **What the law says.** Iowa and California limit an operator to $1,000 a day from each customer and cap its charges at the greater of $5 or 15 percent. At least five US states banned the machines in 2026: Indiana (March 9), Tennessee and Vermont (July 1), Minnesota (August 1) and Hawaii (October 1, for machines that take dollars).

Do not start here. If anyone sends you to one, stop: a caller, a message, someone you have only met online, or a voice that says it is your bank, the police or a relative in trouble. The FTC says real businesses and government agencies will never do that, "and anyone who asks is a scammer." For careful ways to start, see [If You Decide to Hold Some](/journey/using-bitcoin).
