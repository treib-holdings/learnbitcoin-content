> **Where you're going:** Which wallet to use for real bitcoin: a phone app for a small amount, a hardware wallet for more. Four names, checked on November 18, 2026, then how to make sure the one you get is the real one.

## A phone for a little, a device for more

A wallet keeps your keys, and there are two kinds.

A **wallet app** is a program on your phone. It is usually free, always with you, and quick to set up. But a phone is online all day and runs many other apps, and anyone who gets into it can reach the keys. So treat a wallet app like the cash in your pocket: right for an amount you could afford to lose. You will hear this called a hot wallet.

A **hardware wallet** is a small device that keeps your keys away from the internet. A payment goes out only after you approve it on the device itself. It costs about $100 to $250. Once you hold more than you would carry as cash, it is worth the money. You will hear this called a cold wallet.

## The four

**For your phone**

- **BlueWallet**, from [bluewallet.io](https://bluewallet.io/). Free, for iPhone and Android.

**Hardware wallets**

- **Trezor Safe 5 or Safe 7, Bitcoin-only**, from trezor.io: the [Safe 5](https://trezor.io/trezor-safe-5-bitcoin-only) or the [Safe 7](https://trezor.io/trezor-safe-7-bitcoin-only). With an iPhone, take the Safe 7: the Safe 5 needs a computer or an Android phone.
- **Jade Core or Jade Plus**, from [blockstream.com/jade](https://blockstream.com/jade). To unlock, it normally has to reach a computer its maker runs.
- **BitBox02 Nova, Bitcoin-only edition**, from [shop.bitbox.swiss](https://shop.bitbox.swiss/en/). You choose the edition when you order.

Nobody paid to be listed. Fake wallet apps and tampered devices exist, so never search a store or the web for one of these. Start from the links above, and read on for how to check what you get.

## BlueWallet: get the real app

- **Maker's website:** [bluewallet.io](https://bluewallet.io/)
- **Works on:** iPhone and Android. Free. It also comes in Spanish.
- **In the App Store since** May 2018, on Google Play since November 2018.
- **Last checked:** November 18, 2026.

Each store prints the developer, its word for whoever made an app. On your phone it must read exactly:

- iPhone (App Store): `Bluewallet Services, S. R. L.` (a small w, and a comma)
- Android (Google Play): `BlueWallet Services S. R. L.` (a capital W, and no comma)

In order: open the maker's website on your phone and read the address your browser shows. Tap its link to your phone's store. Check the developer's name. Install. Then make a new wallet, write its 12 words on paper, and test the paper before any coins go in. Each step is below.

1. **On the phone that will hold the wallet, open the maker's website** from this page's link, or type it. Before you tap, read this page's own address: learnbitcoin.com. When the maker's site has loaded, read the address your browser shows. It must be bluewallet.io exactly: nothing joined on in front, and no other ending. Never start from a search result, an advertisement or a link in a message.
2. **Tap its link to your phone's store.** It must open the phone's own store app, not a web page dressed as the store: the store app shows no web address at the top. A real install never asks for a file, a profile (a file that changes your phone's settings) or a change in Settings.
3. **Read the developer's name before you install.** On Google Play it is just under the app's title. In the App Store that line is a short description: find the word Developer in the row of facts below, or scroll to Information and read Seller (in some countries, Provider). The Copyright line there spells the name a third way. It is not the check.
4. **Two free checks.** The real app shows 1M+ downloads on Google Play, and years of Version History in the App Store. If yours shows less, stop. A fake can have a history too: these two add to the name check and do not replace it.
5. **Ignore other apps** the store shows.
6. **If the name is different, or the store opened as a web page, stop.** Do not install, and do not search the store. Close it and start again from step 1. Nothing is lost, because nothing is installed.

**Updates** come only through your phone's store. Nobody calls, emails or messages you about one, and no update asks for your words.

**If real words went into something you now doubt,** make a new wallet with new words by these steps, at once, and move the coins to it. Nobody who contacts you can do it for you, and nobody else gives you words or an address.

### When you first open it

1. **Make a new wallet of the kind called Bitcoin,** not {'Multisig Vault'} (a wallet with several keys) or Lightning (a second payment network). This course has not covered those.
2. **Make it new: tap Create.** Bring in no words you had before, and none that anyone gave you. Never use the practice wallet's words for real bitcoin. Only the 12 words this app makes for you ever go into it, in step 4.
3. **Write the 12 new words on paper before anything else.**
4. **Test the paper before any coins go in,** as you practiced. Write down the whole address under Receive. Remove the empty wallet (Delete Wallet). Bring it back from the paper, in the same app (Import wallet). The same address, to the last character, means the paper is right. If the app refuses the words, or the address differs, the paper is wrong: remove any wallet that came back, destroy that paper and start again with a new wallet. Do this once, yourself: nobody will ever ask you to. Never try it on a wallet that holds coins.

### Good to know

- **Do not tap Buy Bitcoin.** By the app's published code, the program text anyone can read, this button opens a bitcoin seller's website and passes it your wallet's next address. This site names no seller: [If You Decide to Hold Some](/journey/using-bitcoin) shows how to choose one from a regulator's list.
- **Reports.** By its privacy policy, the app sends its maker crash reports and basic usage data until you turn on Disable Analytics.
- **Do not tap Speed Up or Cancel yet.** They change a payment that is still waiting. A safety fix makes the app check what another computer tells it first. The maker finished it on November 7, 2026, and it is not in the stores yet. It will come as an ordinary update in your phone's store. Until this page says it has arrived, tap neither: a slow payment can wait.
- **On Google Play,** a box called About the developer shows a person's name, not the company. That box is not the check: the name under the app's title is.

**That is all you need for the phone app.** Two rules from further down belong here too: "support" that contacts you first is not support, and anything that asks for the words of a wallet you already have is theft. The rest of this page is about hardware wallets, what to stay away from, a wallet for a computer, how the list was made, and what this page cannot tell you.

## Hardware wallets: before you buy, and at set-up

The eight tests further down were written for apps: each device is here on the same lines, and only if you do what its paragraph says.

- **Buy only from the maker's own shop.** In 2026 Trezor found three of its devices, sold by an approved shop, with an added part that copies the owner's words. Trezor says none of the three owners lost coins.
- **Never use a device that arrives set up, or with words in the box.** It makes its own: put them on paper, under the four rules of [Keys: What You Actually Hold](/journey/be-your-own-bank), and test them before any coins go in.
- **No check that a device is genuine asks for your words.**
- **Get the device's own app from the maker's website,** never by searching. On a phone, the same way as above. On a computer the maker's website gives you a file: take it from nowhere else. Take the device's updates only inside that app, never from an email or a link. Each maker's app also offers to buy, sell or swap coins. You need none of that.
- **For help, open the maker's address yourself.** Makers' customer and newsletter lists leaked more than once in 2026. That touches no coins, but brings calls, messages and letters posing as the maker. Answer none.

**Trezor Safe 5 or Safe 7, Bitcoin-only,** from trezor.io: the [Safe 5](https://trezor.io/trezor-safe-5-bitcoin-only) or the [Safe 7](https://trezor.io/trezor-safe-7-bitcoin-only). Their usual backup is 20 words of another kind. For 12 or 24, choose Legacy wallet backup types on a computer, or More options and then Legacy options on a phone (the app's names for the older kinds; its help pages say Legacy Backup Types) at set-up. The Safe 5 needs a computer or an Android phone, not an iPhone. The Safe 7 costs more, connects without a cable and works with an iPhone too. In June 2026 Trezor reported that another maker's researchers had got some secrets out of one of the Safe 7's secure chips. The attack needs the device in hand and taken apart, and no update can fully fix it. Trezor says it opens no way to your PIN, your coins or your backup.

**Jade Core or Jade Plus,** from [blockstream.com/jade](https://blockstream.com/jade): 12 ordinary words. At set-up, run its Genuine Check. Update its firmware, the software inside: before set-up on a computer, right after on a phone. To unlock, it normally has to reach a computer its maker runs, through an app. The maker says that computer learns nothing about your wallet. It also handles Liquid, a network this course does not use.

**BitBox02 Nova, Bitcoin-only edition,** from [shop.bitbox.swiss](https://shop.bitbox.swiss/en/). Choose that edition when you order: it cannot be changed later. Use the maker's set-up without the memory card: on a computer, under Advanced options, turn on Skip microSD card backup. You write 24 words and the device checks them. The card is a file, and the course's rule is paper. If you use one all the same, treat it exactly as the paper, and never put it into a phone or computer.

## What to stay away from

- **A "wallet" that never offers you seed words.** Most likely a company holds the keys.
- **A wallet in a web page, or added to a browser, for real bitcoin.** In August 2026 researchers tied 77 add-ons for one browser to one theft operation.
- **Anything that asks for the words of a wallet you already have,** to "connect", "verify", "sync" or "repair" it. That is theft. Real words go into one place only: your own wallet, on its own screen, when you set it up, test your paper or bring it back yourself.
- **"Support" that contacts you first,** or a phone number from a search or a message.

## On a computer

For a reader without a smartphone: Sparrow, a wallet program for Windows, Mac and Linux, from [sparrowwallet.com](https://sparrowwallet.com/). Its maker describes it as suited to advanced users, though not hard to use. It passed tests 1 to 5 and 8 in the list below. Tests 6 and 7 are about app stores, and Sparrow is in none: one named developer signs each release, the last on October 29, 2026.

In a lawsuit filed against Apple in July 2026, three people say a fake app under Sparrow's name took more than $1.8 million from them. Sparrow's download page shows how to check that your file is real.

## How the list was made

Other apps were looked at. Most are good at what they are built for, but each missed a test for a first wallet.

1. You hold the keys.
2. Its code is published.
3. Bitcoin only.
4. Ordinary seed words, shown when the wallet is made, that work in other wallets too.
5. No account.
6. A named maker (each store prints a company name) and an update in the last six months.
7. In both stores, linked from the maker's website.
8. Your words stay on your phone unless you choose otherwise.

BlueWallet passed all eight, by what its maker and the stores publish, with two cautions. The App Store's version is one step ahead of the newest version marked in its published code (test 2). And Google Play's box called About the developer shows a person's name where a company's would be expected (test 6). The software itself was not tested.

## What this page cannot tell you

- **That a wallet has no flaws.** You cannot see how it makes its seed words. In 2026 published code did not stop a flaw in the devices of a maker not on this list, and no update mends words made badly.
- **That your app matches the published code.** For iPhone apps, a project that checks this says nobody has managed it.
- **What a store shows tomorrow.** If the Last checked date above is over six months old, take more care.

A wallet that fails a test comes off, and the [corrections page](/corrections) says so. That alone puts no coins in danger. If there is ever something to do, the corrections page will say what. Nobody will contact you.

This list lives only at learnbitcoin.com. The site sends it to nobody, and no wallet may call itself approved by it.

## What this buys you

One careful way to a real wallet. The list will change, and this page will say when.
